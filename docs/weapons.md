# Weapons

Weapon design for Open's Kenshi-inspired, order-driven combat. This document is a design discussion, not an implementation plan.

- **Current** — present in the game now.
- **Locked** — agreed target design; not necessarily implemented.
- **Locked elsewhere** — already decided in `characters.md` or `combat.md`.
- **Proposed** — a direction to discuss, not a rule to implement yet.
- **Open** — needs a decision before implementation.

## Current and boundaries

`characters.md` owns attributes, skills, equipment slots and body-part health. `combat.md` owns combat orders, range, cooldowns, defense, damage and combat XP. This file describes weapons and their properties. C++ owns gameplay; Godot presents the result and sends intent.

The melee slice is the typed one: weapon properties come from a validated definition catalog, the player owns the five starter instances (definitions 101-105) in a real backpack with quality, durability, limb gates and hand choice, techniques and the three workbench actions run in C++, and damage carries per-component armour mitigation. The scene presents it: click-to-move, held models, attack motion, damage numbers, and the equipment, technique and workbench panels. Design ideas below are not implemented merely because they appear here.

The target principle is: weapon properties + character attributes + weapon proficiency + attack technique determine combat effectiveness. A character gives an attack order; attacks are automatic, not manually selected each swing. Player and NPC use the same rules.

## Weapon identity and skill — locked elsewhere

A weapon belongs to a currently defined skill family: One-Handed Weapons, Two-Handed Weapons or Polearms, with matching advanced options in `characters.md`. A chosen matching specialization overrides its parent for that job; other jobs use the frozen parent. A newly chosen specialization starts at rank 1 with zero XP. Unarmed Combat is separate from a held weapon.

Each weapon has exactly one skill family and, where applicable, one matching specialization. For example, a one-handed sword belongs to One-Handed Weapons and matches Sword. It follows the existing specialization and frozen-parent rules above; no new skills are implied.

Each weapon is defined as either one-handed or two-handed. Handedness is separate from its skill family: a polearm, for example, retains the Polearms family. Held slots and disabled-arm restrictions remain as specified in `characters.md`. Definitions 101–105 exist, and their families, matching specializations and handedness are locked in Starter playable equipment below; how the tags are represented in code is an implementation choice, not a design question. Which weapons exist beyond the starter slice, and their handedness, remain Open. Do not treat the list of skill specializations as a finished item catalog.

### Starter equipment interaction — locked for now

The character sheet provides an Equipment popup listing the player character's owned items and showing what is equipped. The player chooses the left or right hand when equipping a one-handed item; a two-handed weapon occupies both hands. If a required slot is occupied, reject the equip and ask the player to unequip the occupying item first; do not automatically swap. The display-model popup remains separate, and clicking scenery never creates an item. If two one-handed weapons are equipped, attack with the right-hand weapon; if it is unusable, use the left-hand weapon. A shield never attacks; no dual-wield attacks or bonuses for now. Normal ownership and limb restrictions still apply.

### Starter combat presentation — locked for now

Show equipped starter weapon and shield models in the character's hands, play a visible attack motion, and display damage with enough decimal precision that small positive hits are visible. Presentation reflects C++ simulation state and does not determine combat results.

**Current.** Equipped meshes parent to `BoneAttachment3D` sockets on the `hand_l` / `hand_r` bones, created on first equip, and follow them through idle, walk and attack. Each starter model has an explicit local grip (offset, rotation, scale) — presentation tuning awaiting a desktop polish pass. A performed swing plays the mapped skeletal clip from `take_combat_events`, time-warped to that swing's own reported attack-cooldown duration; a nullified or zero-damage performed swing animates the same way, and a refused attempt reports nothing to animate. The whole-body `Visual` tilt is gone. Standing idle takes `Sword_Idle` with a usable weapon, `Idle_Shield` with a shield only, and the exported idle otherwise; walking keeps `Walk`. Damage numbers follow C++ events. One gate stays open: suppressing attack motion on a disabled arm is not yet side-specific — `held_usable` still works off either surviving arm for a one-handed item, presentation animates every performed event as locked, and the side-specific rule is deferred by decision, not by oversight.

**Locked — hand bones.** Equipped weapon and shield meshes parent to a `BoneAttachment3D` on the matching hand bone (`hand_r` / `hand_l`), the same way hair already follows `Head`. They stay attached through idle, walk and attack, not only during a swing. Which hand still comes from C++: a one-handed item follows the slot it is equipped in. A two-handed item is one mesh, parented to `hand_r` when both slots are reserved. Each starter model has a presentation-only local grip (offset, rotation, scale) so the handle sits in the palm; those transforms do not change reach or hit detection and are tuned against the live Quaternius OBJ orientation. Do not invent new weapon meshes. Equipping and unequipping attach or free the mesh on the next presentation tick; there is no draw or sheathe clip. Scenery inspection stays scenery. A disabled arm keeps the mesh and suppresses attack motion, as already locked. Standing idle with a usable weapon uses the library clip `Sword_Idle` instead of `Idle_FoldArms`; standing idle with only a shield uses `Idle_Shield` (imported name may drop `_Loop`). Walking keeps the current `Walk` clip.

**Locked — attack clips.** A performed swing reported by `take_combat_events` plays one skeletal attack clip, including blocked, armour-zero and zero-damage attempts. A refused attempt plays none. Do not start a swing from a click, a cooldown timer, or a visual guess. The whole-body Visual tilt is replaced. Animation length does not own the attack clock; C++ still does. Clip map, using only clips already in the two Quaternius libraries: basic attack and Slash (technique 1) use `Sword_Regular_A`; Thrust (technique 2) uses `Sword_Regular_B` (no dedicated thrust clip exists); Heavy Strike (technique 3) uses `Sword_Heavy_Combo`; unarmed uses `Punch_Jab`. The same map is used for sword, dagger, spear and hammer. `technique_id` 0 is the basic clip. Playback is time-warped so the clip's duration matches that swing's C++ attack cooldown, including the exhausted doubling when that swing was unpaid. Damage numbers appear when the event fires, as they do now; they are not delayed to a contact frame and no wind-up is added to the simulation.

**Locked — this polish's boundary.** Block, dodge, hit-react, stun, death, two-hand IK on the spear shaft, and new animation assets are out of this slice. Defense has no combat event to drive a clip from.

### Unarmed fallback — locked for now

When there is no usable weapon, use the existing Unarmed Combat skill and specialization rules with 0 cut, 0 pierce and 8 blunt base damage, maximum horizontal reach 1.5, no minimum reach and base stamina cost 8. There are no weapon requirements, weapon durability or weapon techniques for an unarmed attack. The normal trained-skill damage, cooldown, exhaustion, defense, armour and Toughness rules still apply.

These describe the intended design space, not finalized units, ranges, formulas or an assertion that every effect exists today. What the starter slice has already settled is locked in Starter playable equipment, Damage types and armour, Weapon blocking, Character handling, Materials, quality and durability, and Starter workbenches and materials; read a remaining "decisions still needed" cell below as reaching past the starter slice.

## Damage types and armour — locked target; formulas settled for the starter slice

For the first playable slice, implement and test the armour mechanics using test fixtures, but do not add playable armour items or invent armour models. Playable armour content is deferred.

Each weapon has nonnegative cut, pierce and blunt damage values. Any component may be zero, but a usable weapon must have at least one component above zero. A single attack can deal a mixture of the three. Armour resists each component separately; the remaining components are added together into one hit against the selected body part. Apply that total to the part and main HP through the existing character damage rules.

For each attack, apply block or dodge first. A ready defender still attempts the selected defense against zero offensive damage, spending the normal stamina cost if affordable and starting the defense cooldown; the existing exhaustion rules apply if unpaid. Since no damage is prevented, this grants no defense XP, Wall progress or block wear. If defense prevents the entire attack, select no body part and consume no body-part random draw. Otherwise select one of the six existing body parts uniformly before checking its armour; even if armour then absorbs all damage, the selected part remains the attack's selected part. A zero-offense attempt selects no body part. For each hit, use at most one armour piece: Head → Head armour; Torso → Chest; either arm → Hands; either leg → Legs. Feet armour remains equipped but provides no mitigation until a separate foot hit location is defined. Armour pieces do not overlap or combine for a hit.

Before armour, multiply each damage component by the relevant trained weapon skill rank / 200, using the same skill and specialization selection as `combat.md`. Do not substitute effective skill. If stamina is too low to pay for the attack, halve each offensive damage component before armour; the attack still happens and its cooldown doubles within the existing speed limits. The three starter technique modifiers are locked in Starter techniques below; modifiers for techniques beyond them remain Open.

The current melee slice uses the all-or-nothing block/dodge test in `combat.md`. A ready dodge automatically succeeds, mitigating all attack damage normally or 50% when exhausted; it then starts the independent defense cooldown. A dodge that prevents any damage before armour grants 1 XP to Dodging or the chosen matching dodge specialization; one that prevents no damage grants none. Prevention is the difference between incoming components and post-dodge components, even if armour would otherwise absorb the hit. After an exhausted partial dodge, the attacker gains 1 weapon-skill XP if damage reaches the target, following the existing damage-based XP rule. A character cannot dodge while that cooldown is running. Dodge cooldown uses the existing combat speed formula and global limits with trained Dodging or the chosen matching dodge specialization. The future design replaces weapon and shield blocks with percentage damage mitigation. Do not implement the old all-or-nothing block as the target rule.

Each armour piece has separate cut, pierce and blunt resistance percentages, each in the range 0% to 100%. Each percentage reduces only its matching damage component. For example, before penetration, 50% cut resistance reduces 10 cut damage to 5. Each weapon has separate cut, pierce and blunt penetration values, each in the range 0 to 100 percentage points; there is no universal penetration. Each value subtracts percentage points only from its matching resistance, with a floor of 0%: 20 cut penetration against 50% cut resistance leaves 30%. Interactions for techniques beyond the three starters remain Open.

After each component is reduced by its matching armour resistance and the components are summed, the defender's **base Toughness** provides a universal reduction to the total, even when unarmoured. Weapon penetration applies only to armour resistance; it cannot penetrate or reduce Toughness.

Toughness reduction (locked for now; coefficient may be balanced later):

`damage after Toughness = damage after armour / (1 + 0.05 × defender base Toughness)`

This gives diminishing returns and does not use the level-multiplied effective attribute.

Techniques can favor different components: a slash may favor cut and a thrust may favor pierce. The starter multipliers are locked in Starter techniques below; effects for other techniques remain Open. No extra accuracy/crit roll is implied.

**Current:** the typed model in this document is what `cpp/` runs. The scalar `max(0, weapon × trained/200 − defender base Toughness × armour)` that `combat.md` used to recite, and its all-or-nothing defense test, were replaced in one deliberate change with no selectable old/new combat mode: mixed damage with per-component armour resistance, percentage mitigation, a defense that pays its own stamina, durability with wear, and stagger from final blunt. Techniques and the workbench actions have since landed on top of that switch: the three rank-1 starter techniques run inside the one attack pipeline, and the smithing and enchanting benches work through the shared queue with materials paid at the start.

## Techniques and range — locked for the starter slice; proposed beyond it

Each weapon defines the techniques it can use, such as slashes, thrusts and overhead strikes. Techniques modify the weapon's existing cut, pierce and blunt damage components rather than defining an entirely separate weapon damage profile. All damage penalties that apply to the weapon's attack also apply to techniques, including requirement effectiveness, exhaustion, minimum-range and zero-durability penalties. The starter modifiers are locked in Starter techniques below; modifiers beyond them remain Open. A character can unlock techniques by reaching a required trained rank in the relevant weapon skill; titles may also grant techniques. The weapon must support the technique in either case. Titles being able to grant techniques is locked and must be covered by test fixtures; the starter slice assigns none, and other unlock conditions and title-granted moves remain Open. A technique can only be performed while the character has a usable weapon that supports it. If the weapon becomes unusable, existing combat rules determine the fallback. NPCs randomly select among ready, applicable unlocked techniques; if none are ready, they use a basic attack. A player without a configured sequence uses the same random selection: choose among ready, applicable unlocked techniques, or use a basic attack if none are ready. The player opens technique configuration from the character sheet. The popup provides controls to enable or disable techniques, add and reorder unlocked techniques, set each sequence entry to Skip or Wait, and view each technique's cooldown. Execution remains automatic during combat; there is no click per swing. When techniques are disabled for the player-controlled character, they still perform a basic attack using the weapon's normal damage values. The configured player sequence loops back to the beginning after its last technique and continues while the attack order is active. Each configured technique has an option to skip it if it is on cooldown or wait until its cooldown finishes. When waiting, the character uses basic attacks while keeping that technique as the next in the sequence. After a technique is performed, advance to the next entry, looping at the end. The player may include duplicate entries to repeat a technique. Skip entries unsupported by the equipped weapon and entries configured to skip while on cooldown. Also skip entries that are no longer unlocked or usable without deleting them; they may become usable again later. Editing a sequence restarts it at its first entry without changing technique cooldowns. Switching between player and NPC control does not reset cooldowns or erase the player's sequence. Check at most one full sequence per attack; if all entries are skipped, use a basic attack. Technique cooldowns belong to the character, not the weapon item; swapping weapons does not reset them. There are no shared technique cooldowns: performing a technique starts only that technique's cooldown. A technique's cooldown starts when it is performed, even if the attack is blocked or deals no damage. Performing a technique also uses the normal attack cooldown and remains within the existing attack-speed limits. Technique attacks follow the existing combat XP rule: a hit that deals damage grants 1 XP to the weapon skill used; zero damage grants none. There is no separate technique XP counter. When all techniques are on cooldown and unavailable, the character uses a basic attack instead. Sequence edge cases, cooldown values, popup details, eligibility and effects beyond what Starter techniques and the rules above settle remain Open — that is, past the starter slice, not inside it.

## Character handling — locked target for the starter slice

The attack cooldown belongs to the character; equipping, removing or swapping weapons does not reset its remaining time. Each weapon has an attack-speed multiplier from 0.1 to 2 inclusive; use neutral 1 for now. Multiply the existing skill-based attack rate by it; 1 leaves the rate unchanged. Continue to enforce the existing global cooldown band of 1/3 to 5 seconds, including the existing exhausted-cooldown rule. Every starter weapon uses speed 1, locked in Starter playable equipment above; values for other weapons remain Open.

Weapon weight is an item property used by the future encumbrance system. It does not apply an additional automatic handling penalty on top of the weapon's explicit requirements, speed and stamina cost. The encumbrance formula remains Open.

Each weapon has a stamina cost per attack, replacing the current placeholder cost of 8. A technique can specify its own stamina cost, replacing the weapon's normal cost for that attack; the existing effectiveness adjustment and exhaustion rules still apply. If the character cannot pay the full cost, the attack still happens and the existing exhaustion penalties apply. Starter weapon costs are 8, 6, 8 and 8 for definitions 101–104, and each starter technique uses its weapon's normal cost, both locked above; costs for other weapons and techniques remain Open.

Every weapon has an attribute requirement and a skill requirement. Heavy weapons require Strength; light weapons require Dexterity. Depending on the enchantment type, an enchanted weapon also gains a Profanity or Intelligence requirement. Attribute requirements always check the character's effective attribute (base × level multiplier × blessing); level and blessing are not separate requirements. The skill requirement checks the relevant trained weapon rank, using the same basic or matching specialization as combat, not effective skill. Meeting all requirements makes the weapon 100% usable. A character can still use a weapon below its requirements, but effectiveness falls with how far their relevant attributes and skills are below the requirements. For each individual requirement, effectiveness is `min(character value / required value, 1)²`: half gives 25%, while meeting or exceeding it gives 100%. Overall weapon effectiveness is the lowest individual effectiveness among all applicable requirements; the largest shortfall is the limiting factor. Below full effectiveness, attack damage and attack speed decrease, while stamina usage increases. Multiply each offensive damage component by overall weapon effectiveness before armour, in addition to the trained-skill multiplier. Multiply the skill- and weapon-adjusted attack rate by overall effectiveness, then apply the existing cooldown limits; if effectiveness is zero, use the slowest permitted cooldown without dividing by zero. Exhaustion still doubles the resulting cooldown and reclamps it. Stamina cost is `base attack cost × (2 − overall effectiveness)`, where the base is the technique cost when specified, otherwise the weapon cost: full effectiveness leaves cost unchanged, while zero effectiveness doubles it. Every declared requirement value must be greater than zero; an inapplicable requirement is absent rather than zero. Equipping is not forbidden for falling short; existing limb-use gates still apply.

Strength, Dexterity, Agility and proficiency may affect weapon handling, within rules to be decided. Current combat uses the relevant **trained rank**, not effective skill, for damage and cooldown. Effective attributes include the main-level multiplier (up to 100), while HP does not; any new attribute influence needs an explicit bounded rule to avoid uncontrolled scaling. Do not silently change skill progression, pool formulas, limb gates, the cooldown band or exhaustion behavior.

## Weapon blocking — locked target

Each weapon and shield has a block mitigation percentage from 0% to 100%. A block reduces each incoming damage component by its mitigation percentage before armour and Toughness. Weapon armour penetration does not reduce or bypass block mitigation. A good shield can have 100% mitigation; a low-quality one can have 50% (examples, not tier formulas). Weapon block efficiency should be much lower than shield block efficiency. At zero durability, a weapon's or shield's block mitigation is 33% of its original value: e.g. 30% becomes 9.9%. The defender keeps an independent defense cooldown on the character, not on equipment; equipping, removing or swapping items does not reset it. Switching between block and dodge preserves the same remaining cooldown; the newly selected defense is available when that cooldown reaches zero. When ready, they block and start the clock; while it is cooling down, incoming attacks are not blocked. Block cooldown uses the existing combat speed formula and global cooldown limits, using trained Blocking or the matching shield specialization. A block that prevents any damage before armour grants 1 XP to the Blocking skill used and counts toward the Wall title; one that prevents no damage grants neither. Prevention is measured as the difference between incoming components and post-block components, even if armour would otherwise absorb the hit. A block with 0% mitigation can still be selected but prevents no damage and grants no Blocking XP. After a partial block, an attacker still gains 1 weapon-skill XP if the hit deals damage to the target, following the existing damage-based XP rule. A shield loses 1 durability point for a block that prevents damage for now; this amount is tunable separately from weapon wear. A block uses the blocking item's durability at its start; if wear reduces the shield or weapon to zero, the durability penalty starts on the next block. Defense selection: if the character has a shield equipped, use the shield. Without a shield, a character using a weapon chooses weapon block or dodge by comparing the effective values of the relevant Blocking and Dodging skills, including applicable specializations; without a weapon, dodge. If an attack, block or dodge cannot be fully paid, the action still happens while exhausted and spends no stamina; unpaid blocks and dodges follow the same logic as attacks. An exhausted attack has half offensive damage; an exhausted block has half its normal mitigation and an exhausted dodge mitigates 50% of attack damage. The applicable cooldown doubles and is reclamped to the global limits. Block and dodge each cost 8 stamina for now; their costs are independently tunable. This replaces the former shield-versus-dodge higher-skill comparison. For a weapon-block versus dodge skill tie, choose dodge. A shield at zero durability is still used, with its block mitigation reduced to 33% of normal. If also exhausted, apply both penalties: halve the reduced mitigation. The same stacking applies to a zero-durability weapon used to block. If an arm makes the equipped shield unusable, ignore that shield and apply the weapon-block versus dodge logic using any usable weapon. Existing limb gates still apply.

## Stagger — locked direction

Stagger buildup uses the calculated final blunt damage component after armour and Toughness reductions, even if that component exceeds remaining HP: each point adds one point to a stagger meter. Apply the hit once through existing HP clamping; if it kills the target, do not apply a new stun to the dead character. Attack XP follows the existing damage-based rule, including lethal hits: a positive-damage hit on a living target grants 1 weapon-skill XP even if it kills that target. No further hit on a dead target grants XP. The meter triggers an interruption when it reaches the threshold:

`stagger threshold = 10 × defender effective Toughness`

Effective Toughness is base Toughness × the level multiplier × the Toughness blessing, as defined in `characters.md`. The coefficient 10 is a starting balance value. Damage reduction still uses base Toughness only. A damaging hit can build stagger toward an interruption; an attack fully negated by defense adds none. For a partial block or exhausted partial dodge, only blunt damage that gets through the defense, armour and Toughness contributes to stagger. When stagger triggers, the meter resets to zero and the character is stunned for 2 seconds for now. Stagger buildup and recovery are paused throughout the stun, so blunt hits during it do not fill the meter or refresh the stun; both resume after the stun ends. While stunned, they cannot move, attack or defend, and armour mitigation is 0% for every attack: only Toughness mitigates incoming damage. Attack, defense and technique cooldowns freeze for the duration of the stun and resume counting down after it ends. If a simulation tick crosses the end of a stun, only the portion after the stun ends advances those cooldowns. Their current order remains and resumes after the stun. Equipped armour is not removed; its mitigation resumes when the stun ends. Between hits, buildup decreases by 10% of the total stagger meter (the threshold) per second for now, down to zero. Apply recovery for elapsed time before adding a new hit's blunt damage. Other stun sources and their interactions remain Open.

If effective Toughness changes, recalculate the threshold and check whether the meter reaches it on the next damaging hit, not immediately upon the attribute change. There is no artificial stagger-threshold cap. On death, clear the stagger meter and stun; a dead character cannot resume an order.

### Simultaneous events — locked for now

After stun expires, the character can act on the next eligible combat update. If combat starts at exactly the instant an in-progress workbench action would complete, the combat interruption takes precedence: the action fails and its materials are consumed. When characters attack in the same update, resolve attacks in the existing roster order; a character killed or stunned by an earlier hit cannot perform a later attack in that update.

## Damage and injuries — locked for now

Weapon hits cause normal damage through the existing body-part and main-HP rules. No bleeding or fractures for now. The existing zero-HP limb debuffs remain; the separately locked blunt-damage stagger and stun rules are unchanged. Other injury conditions are not part of this design for now.

## Materials, quality and durability — locked for the starter slice

A weapon's material determines its baseline properties; craftsmanship determines its quality and modifies those properties. Weapon quality has 7 tiers, numbered 1 through 7, with no tier names defined. Quality is fixed at creation and cannot be changed afterward; repair does not upgrade it. The crafter's relevant Smithing or Weaponsmith skill determines their maximum craftable quality using these locked seven-tier bands. The highest tier requires advanced rank greater than 190:

| Skill | Trained ranks | Maximum quality |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Smithing | 1–34 | 1 |
| Basic Smithing | 35–67 | 2 |
| Basic Smithing | 68–100 | 3 |
| Advanced Weaponsmith | 1–63 | 4 |
| Advanced Weaponsmith | 64–127 | 5 |
| Advanced Weaponsmith | 128–190 | 6 |
| Advanced Weaponsmith | 191–200 | 7 |

A crafter may deliberately produce any lower weapon quality tier they have unlocked instead of their highest available tier. Successfully crafting a weapon counts as one item toward Hammerhand. Repairing or enchanting does not count toward Hammerhand; failed or cancelled work grants no title progress. Choosing Weaponsmith allows advanced quality for weapons only, not armour; choosing Armoursmith allows advanced quality for armour only, not weapons. Each specialization permits high-tier crafting only in its own field; the other field uses frozen basic Smithing. For weapons, an Armoursmith's frozen basic Smithing caps crafting at tier 3. The corresponding Armour-specific tier thresholds remain Open. Successfully crafting a weapon grants 1 XP to that skill for now; the amount should be easy to tune later. Weapon crafting requires a suitable workbench and materials and is only available outside combat. Weapon crafting always succeeds when all conditions are met. If combat begins during crafting or the player cancels it, crafting fails: the required materials are consumed, and no weapon or XP is granted. Weapon crafting takes a fixed 15 seconds. Starter recipes are locked in Starter workbenches and materials below; other recipes remain Open. Quality multipliers match character levels by tier: tier 1 ×1, tier 2 ×2, tier 3 ×4, tier 4 ×8, tier 5 ×16, tier 6 ×32 and tier 7 ×100. The multiplier applies only to weapon damage components, not speed, reach, penetration or other weapon properties. The starter material and the starter weapons' damage values are locked in Starter playable equipment and Starter workbenches and materials above; other materials and other weapons remain Open. Each newly created weapon starts at its maximum durability, determined by its material and quality; current durability cannot exceed that maximum. Rules for generating worn or damaged loot remain Open. Weapons lose 1 durability point per attack for now, whether it deals damage or is blocked; this amount should be easy to tune. A weapon also loses 1 durability point for each successful block for now; block wear and attack wear are separately tunable. Weapons can be repaired. Weapon repair uses Smithing, or Weaponsmith when that specialization is chosen, following the existing skill-resolution rules. A repair restores a percentage of the weapon's maximum durability, capped at that maximum. Repair does not permanently lower maximum durability; a weapon can be restored to its original maximum. For now, the percentage restored per repair equals the relevant trained Smithing or Weaponsmith rank, capped at 100% of maximum durability; the formula should be easy to tune. Round the restored amount up to the next whole durability point, then cap at the weapon's maximum. Repairs require materials and a specific workbench, and are unavailable while the character is in combat. A repair takes a fixed 15 seconds. This workbench requirement applies to repair of any equipment, not just weapons. If combat begins during a repair or the player voluntarily cancels it, the repair fails: the required materials are consumed, and it restores no durability or XP. Workbench types, materials and quantities beyond the starter benches and their Scrap costs remain Open. Durability uses whole-number points, clamped from 0 to its material- and quality-determined maximum. The weapon remains usable, and its permanent enchantments remain attached and active at zero durability. For the starter Blunt Edge enchantment, its added damage follows the combined-component formula in the starter workbenches section; interactions for other enchantment damage remain open. ## Starter workbenches and materials — locked temporary content

These are placeholders for the first playable implementation, not final balance. Place one clearly visible Smithing Workbench and one clearly visible Enchanting Workbench in the starter scene. The Smithing Workbench supports the starter weapon recipes and equipment repair; the Enchanting Workbench supports weapon enchantment. Neither grants free or unlimited resources.

Give the player a starting supply of 20 units of Scrap and 5 units of Rune Dust in their owned material storage. At the Smithing Workbench, crafting any one of starter weapon definitions 101–104 costs 2 Scrap; the output is a new instance of that definition at the selected unlocked quality tier. Repairing a damaged starter weapon or shield costs 1 Scrap. Starter shield repair uses basic Smithing for both the repair percentage and XP, not Weaponsmith. Armour-specialization repair rules remain open. Weapon crafting and repair use the existing 15-second, skill, quality, success, XP and interruption rules above. These are not shield-crafting recipes or armour-crafting rules.

The starter Enchanting Workbench offers one temporary enchantment, Blunt Edge, for any weapon: it adds blunt damage equal to `trained Enchanting or Weapon enchant rank × 0.01`, fixed at application, and adds an Intelligence requirement of `ceil(trained rank / 20)` per application. It costs 1 Rune Dust and one enchantment slot. Same-type effects and requirements add under the existing enchantment rules. Enchanting uses the existing 15-second, eligibility, success, XP and interruption rules above. For the starter Blunt Edge enchantment, add its fixed blunt damage after applying quality to base weapon damage, then apply the normal trained-skill, requirement-effectiveness, exhaustion, minimum-range, durability and applicable technique modifiers to the combined blunt component. At zero durability its added damage is reduced to 33% along with weapon damage; the enchantment remains attached and active.

Workbench transaction rules: validate all requirements before starting; an invalid request consumes nothing. On a valid start, consume required materials immediately and exactly once; completion, cancellation and interruption do not consume them again. Capture the relevant trained skill at the start for the action's result. The character must stay within workbench reach; leaving reach, dying, losing the target item or losing the workbench interrupts the action without returning consumed materials. Existing combat-interruption and voluntary-cancellation rules still apply.

The workbench interaction range is 1.5 horizontal world units, inclusive. Clicking a workbench opens its action popup. If the character is outside the 1.5-unit interaction range, clicking the bench issues an automatic movement order to approach it. The popup shows available actions, material costs and progress, with a Cancel button. If the player selects an action while approaching, queue it and start it automatically on arrival. Validate requirements and consume materials only when the action actually starts. Before it starts, a new movement or attack order, combat beginning (including an incoming attack order), cancellation, or the bench disappearing clears the queue without consuming materials. If requirements are not met on arrival, the queued action fails without consuming materials. Issuing a movement order interrupts an active action even before leaving range. Equipping or transferring the target item is rejected while the action is active; the player may finish or cancel first. Other recipes or enchantments remain open. Do not treat these placeholders as implemented or as approval for a larger catalog.

## Enchanting — proposed, with the starter enchantment locked

Weapons should be enchantable through the existing Enchanting basic skill and its Weapon enchant specialization, respecting the existing one-specialization-per-basic and frozen-parent rules. This is distinct from the weapon skill used when attacking. An enchantment is permanently fixed to the individual weapon: it cannot be removed, replaced or expire. Its effects and added attribute requirement stay with that weapon when traded or equipped by someone else. Enchantment capacity equals weapon quality tier: tier 1 has one slot, through tier 7 with seven slots total. Multiple enchantments of the same type can be stacked on one weapon; their effect values add together rather than multiply. Enchantment attribute requirements also add for each matching attribute: two enchantments requiring 2 Intelligence each require 4 Intelligence in total. Different attributes have separate totals: Intelligence requirements add to Intelligence, and Profanity requirements add to Profanity. Each applicable total is checked against the wielder. The resulting attribute totals are checked alongside the weapon's other applicable requirements using the existing lowest-effectiveness rule. When the base weapon and enchantments require the same attribute, add the base requirement and all enchantment requirements for that attribute; different attributes remain separate checks. Each successful enchantment permanently consumes one available slot. The enchanter's trained Enchanting or matching Weapon enchant rank determines the enchantment's strength when applied. The resulting value is fixed on the weapon; stronger enchantments generally require more of their associated Intelligence or Profanity to wield effectively. The starter Blunt Edge scaling is locked in Starter workbenches and materials above; scaling for other enchantments remains Open. Its attribute requirement is added immediately, and the weapon's effectiveness is recalculated for its wielder using the updated requirements. Enchanting requires a dedicated workbench and materials and is available only outside combat. As with repair, if combat begins during the action or the player voluntarily cancels it, the action fails; the required materials are consumed and no enchantment, slot or XP is granted. Enchanting takes a fixed 15 seconds. Material costs beyond the starter Rune Dust price remain Open. Enchanting always succeeds when all its conditions are met; unmet conditions do not produce a successful enchantment or consume a slot. A successful weapon enchantment grants 1 skill XP to Enchanting or the chosen Weapon enchant specialization, following existing skill resolution. The XP amount is a tunable value, set to 1 for now, rather than a hardcoded assumption in future implementation. This is skill XP, not main-level XP. Enchanting conditions and consequences of unsuccessful attempts beyond the starter slice remain Open: what is settled is that an invalid request consumes nothing and an interrupted or cancelled active action keeps its materials and grants no enchantment, slot or XP. Enchantment effects, costs and interaction with magic damage beyond the locked Blunt Edge and its Rune Dust cost remain Open; the starter workbench is the only crafting implementation this slice builds, and no spell system is implied.
